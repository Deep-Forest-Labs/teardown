# The Altar as a real machine: the Olivier drum revolve

Reference machine: the drum revolve under the National Theatre's Olivier stage (Mole Richardson, mid-1970s).
Source: Light & Sound International, Nov 2017, "National Theatre" (theatrecrafts.com archive), plus ArtsJournal 2013.

## Real numbers used

| Part | Real | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Drum diameter | 12 m | LSI 2017 ("the whole 12m diameter") |
| Mass incl. elevator counterweights | about 160 t | LSI 2017 |
| Elevators | two, semicircular, counterweighted | LSI 2017 |
| Elevator load limit | 5 t each | LSI 2017 |
| Elevator travel | three floors below the stage | theatrecrafts, LSI |
| Height | five storeys | ArtsJournal 2013 |
| Top deck | a half disc on top; pinned to the stage while the drum turns under it, or turning with the drum, or on its own motors | LSI 2017 |
| Wheels under the drum | 70 (after the rebuild) | LSI 2017 |
| Drive motors | 24 | LSI 2017 |
| Core | lattice steelwork | LSI 2017 (manufacturer's photo) |

Estimated (not published): storey height 3.5 m, wall thickness 0.4 m, counterweight guides in the wall, rope
reeving 1:1, the ring gear the 24 motors drive.

Game scale: everything × 2.5 (a 30 m drum), so the proportions stay the real ones.

## The rule the machine imposes

The top half disc covers one half of the circle at stage level. An elevator deck is one rigid half disc, so it can
only come up to stage level when it sits exactly in the uncovered half. Anywhere else it must stay under the top
deck. When the top deck is pinned, the drum therefore indexes in half turns: stop, raise, hold, lower, turn.
When the top deck is free and turns with the drum, an elevator that is up stays up while everything spins.

## How the player and the prince map onto it

| State | Machine | Player |
|---|---|---|
| Run (no prince) | Top deck pinned. The drum indexes 180° at a time; the elevator in the open half rises to stage level, holds, sinks. | Stage ring and the pinned half disc are safe ground; the open half is a pit with a timed bridge. Teaches run and jump. |
| Arrival | Drum stops. Elevator B sinks the full three floors to the basement and comes back up carrying the prince. | The sacrifice rises on the altar deck. |
| Phase 1 | Top deck unpinned: it turns with the drum, so B stays up with the prince; the whole drum becomes a turntable. | Fight on a spinning floor, prince riding with it. |
| Phase 2 | Top deck pinned again, the drum indexes again. The prince's deck must sink before every half turn and can only rise in the open half; the empty elevator is the bridge on the other dwell. | The prince is exposed in alternate dwells; the pit opens and closes under the player. |

The prince's body is not designed here. The deck carries a placeholder load; its shape comes later and must not
change the machine.
